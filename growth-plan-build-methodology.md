# Growth Plan Build Methodology

Everything you need to turn raw discovery-call data into a defensible, persuasive growth plan presentation. Companion to `growth-plan-deck-skill.md` (which covers the template structure) — this file covers the *how* and *why* of building one well.

This skill is for service businesses (agencies, consultants, fractional providers) pitching a prospect with real numbers. It works for one-time engagements, retainers, performance-priced offers, or any mix.

---

## 1. When to invoke

Use this skill when:
- You've done one or more discovery calls with a prospect and you're about to build the deck
- You're re-pitching a prospect with a new offer after a previous proposal missed
- You want the deck to survive scrutiny from a CFO, ops lead, or skeptical founder
- The prospect is paying >$3K/month or >$10K/engagement (if smaller, use a one-page proposal, not a deck)

**Do NOT use this for cold prospects.** A cold prospect hasn't given you enough data. Book a discovery call first.

---

## 2. What you must have before you start

If any of the following is missing, pause and get it. Do not build the deck without these inputs:

| Input | What you're pulling | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| All prior call transcripts | Literal quotes, stated pain, stated goals, objections raised | The deck must sound like *them*, not you |
| Their current numbers | Revenue, close rate, deal size, retention, leads/mo | Every slide number must trace to one of these |
| Market size screenshot | Sales Navigator / Apollo / comparable tool result count | Proof the opportunity is real, not guessed |
| Existing tooling / team | What they already pay for, who already does what | Lets you position as *additive*, not *replacing* |
| Their own words on *risk* | What they said about downside, guarantees, commitments | Directly shapes your risk reversal slide |
| Your offer economics | What it costs *you* to deliver; your margin target | Lets you defend the price if they push |

**Non-negotiable rule:** every number on the deck must trace to one of the above or to a public industry source you can cite. If it doesn't, strip it.

---

## 3. The classic growth plan arc

The template uses a 21-slide persuasion arc proven across consulting, agency, and coaching. Do not rearrange without reason.

```
BLOCK 1 — PAIN       (slides 1-5)   Hook, listen back, gaps, cost, old vs new
BLOCK 2 — SOLUTION   (slide 6)      Your mechanism in one slide
BLOCK 3 — PILLARS    (slides 7-10)  Deep dives + bonuses
BLOCK 4 — PROOF      (slides 11-12) TAM math + case studies
BLOCK 5 — ANCHOR     (slides 13-14) Cost to DIY vs value stack
BLOCK 6 — OFFER      (slides 15-16) Guarantee then price
BLOCK 7 — CLOSE      (slides 17-21) ROI, worst case, Brunson close, timeline, summary
```

The structure maps to the psychology. Don't skip blocks even if you feel a block is "obvious." The reader isn't inside your head.

### What each block needs to do

**Pain (1-5).** By slide 5 the prospect should feel *seen*. They should be thinking "this person actually listened to me." Every claim you make here must be grounded in something they literally said on a prior call.

**Solution (6).** One slide, one mechanism, one promise. Name it. Don't describe the process yet; describe the *outcome* and hint at *how* you're different.

**Pillars (7-10).** Now the process. Break your delivery into 2-4 pillars. Each pillar gets a deep-dive slide. Include at least one screenshot of an actual tool/dashboard per pillar — screenshots sell.

**Proof (11-12).** The TAM math slide connects the promise to the market. The case study slide connects the promise to prior results. If you have no case studies yet, use your own results or a pilot.

**Anchor (13-14).** Slide 13 is what it costs to build in-house (higher than your price). Slide 14 is the itemized value of what they get (also higher than your price). This brackets your price between two bigger numbers on either side.

**Offer (15-16).** Guarantee first, then price. Not the reverse. The guarantee lowers the felt risk so the price feels smaller.

**Close (17-21).** Five slides of "help them say yes." ROI at their own numbers, worst-case scenario (what they keep even if you fail), Brunson's "if all this did was..." close, timeline (what happens on day 1), and a summary with 5 FAQs.

## 4. Number defensibility

Every slide number falls into one of four categories. Mark them in your build doc:

| Tag | Source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `CLIENT` | Prospect told you on a call or in email | "We close 25% of sales meetings" |
| `MARKET` | Public industry benchmark, cite the source | "Cold outbound closes 10-20% (industry band)" |
| `TOOL` | A live screenshot of a tool showing the number | "22,000 prospects match this ICP (Sales Nav)" |
| `OFFER` | Your own pricing and delivery math | "We deliver 8 qualified calls/mo at $X" |

Tag each number on each slide. When the prospect challenges one, you can say exactly where it came from.

**Forbidden:** guessing, rounding up without noting it, using their warm close rate on cold traffic, using industry averages as if they were their specific number.

## 5. TAM validation — screenshot or it didn't happen

On the market-size slide, never put a bare number. Always embed a live screenshot of the search that produced it.

**Sales Navigator pattern:**
1. Build the search that matches the prospect's ICP exactly (title + industry + geography + company size)
2. Note the count (e.g. "22,000+")
3. Screenshot the filter sidebar AND the result count in one image
4. Save as `assets/tam-screenshot.png`
5. Embed on the TAM slide with a caption citing the date

**Apollo pattern:**
1. Build the filter set that matches their ICP
2. Screenshot the count in the top bar
3. Same treatment as Sales Nav

**Sizing guidance** (for cold-outbound offers):
- TAM ≥ 15K: comfortable monthly rotation at 2 segments
- TAM 8-15K: 6-week rotation cycle, smaller monthly volume
- TAM 3-8K: small-list rules apply — 3-email sequences max, 90-day list rest
- TAM < 3K: do NOT sell a pay-per-lead offer against this list, it will burn out in 60 days

**Volume math benchmark for cold email:**
- Emails per qualified call (plan): 2,500
- Emails per qualified call (budget / sequential worst case): 3,000-3,571
- Apply: `monthly_emails / 2500 = expected qualified calls/mo`

## 6. The three-scenario close rate framework

Never pitch a single close rate. Always show three columns on the P&L slide: **Conservative / Expected / Best**.

**Rules for picking the scenarios:**

- **Conservative** = the industry floor for cold outbound with tight qualification. Usually **10-15%**. This is the column that has to survive a CFO audit.
- **Expected** = the prospect's own stated close rate, if they've told you one. If they haven't, use the industry midpoint.
- **Best** = the prospect's warm / referral close rate, if they have one. Don't go higher than this — it reads as fantasy.

**Hard rule:** never put the prospect's warm rate in the Conservative column. A prospect who closes 33% of referrals will *not* close 33% of cold-traffic qualified calls. Expecting them to is how you end up fired in month three.

**Good Conservative anchor language:**
> "At the conservative 15% close (industry floor for cold outbound with tight qualification), you net $X."

If they push on Conservative being too low, you have the Expected and Best columns right there to show the upside.

## 7. The 5 qualification criteria pattern

If you're offering a pay-per-qualified-call structure (or any offer where "qualified" is the trigger for a fee), you **must** pre-define what qualified means. Five criteria is the right number — fewer feels loose, more feels bureaucratic.

Template:
1. **Decision maker present** (exact titles you'll accept)
2. **Target geography** (country / region / state)
3. **Revenue / company-size fit** (exact band)
4. **Expressed relevant pain** (not guessed — they wrote or said something pain-adjacent in the reply)
5. **Showed up and stayed** (on camera, full duration, engaged)

**Operational rules:**
- Written down before week 1, signed off by both sides
- Every booked call tagged against each criterion
- Every call recorded (Fathom, Fireflies, or similar) — replayable
- If the client later believes a call was stretched, pull the tape; refund the per-call fee without arguing

Refunding $X per call is cheap. Losing the relationship over $X is expensive.

## 8. Risk reversal architecture (don't bolt on a guarantee — design one in)

The guarantee isn't a separate slide's worth of marketing copy. It's the structure of the offer.

**Good risk reversal is made of three compounding layers:**

1. **At-cost component** — if some part of the offer is priced at your true supplier cost (with no margin), the prospect knows you aren't bleeding them on the fixed fee.
2. **Performance-only component** — if margin comes from a per-outcome fee (per call, per meeting, per closed deal), you only make money when they do.
3. **Short-notice cancel** — 30-day notice to walk without penalty, any month, no questions.

On top of those three, you *can* add a bonus guarantee (e.g. "X meetings in Y days or month 3 floor waived") but that's optional flair. The architecture IS the reversal.

**Your guarantee slide should name the mechanism, not the marketing hook.** "Infrastructure at cost. Performance only. 30-day cancel." is stronger than "100% satisfaction guaranteed."

## 9. Design system

This skill inherits the design system documented in `growth-plan-deck-skill.md`. Key principles you must not violate:

- **Dark background, single accent color.** Pick one color for your brand (purple, green, blue, anything high-contrast) and apply it via the `--accent` CSS variable. Don't introduce a second accent.
- **Inter 900 for all large numbers.** Serif fonts on numbers look weird; fancy fonts on numbers look untrustworthy.
- **Sharp corners on tables, rounded corners on boxes.** `.line-items` and `.scorecard` get `border-radius: 0`. Everything else uses the `--radius` variable.
- **Full-width layouts.** Every slide uses the same max-width container (1080px is standard). Don't let any slide feel narrower than the others.
- **Ambient orbs for depth.** Large radial-gradient circles at ~10% opacity in slide corners. Purple + green. Don't overdo them.

## 10. Copy rules (non-negotiable)

These rules compound. Break one and the deck starts feeling amateur.

### 10.1 No em dashes
Use commas, periods, or colons. Replace any `—` or `&mdash;` before shipping. Em dashes break scanning flow and draw disproportionate attention.

### 10.2 No widow words (HARD RULE)
Never leave 1-2 orphan words on the last line of a multi-line text block. Three-layer defense:

1. **Shorten the copy first.** Trim until the last line has at least 3 words, or the whole paragraph is one line.
2. **Bind the last 2-3 words with `&nbsp;`.** Example: `building&nbsp;in-house`, `in&nbsp;rotation`, `qualified&nbsp;calls`. Non-breaking spaces prevent the widow.
3. **Add global CSS:** `p, .subtitle, .li-desc, .detail-body p, .card p { text-wrap: pretty; }`. Modern browsers (Chrome 117+, Safari 17.4+) auto-balance the last few lines.

Before shipping, visually scan every multi-line block for a widow. If you see one, fix it.

### 10.3 Under their brand, not yours
The outreach the deck pitches goes out under the *prospect's* name and company, not yours. In the copy, say "you reach out," "your prospects receive," "booked on your calendar." Never say "we contact people as your agency."

### 10.4 "Genuine interest confirmed," not "happy to chat"
The 5th qualification criterion is *genuine interest surfaced in reply* — pain keyword, specific question, meaningful engagement. Don't let the criterion collapse to "they said yes to a call." Anyone will take a free 30 minutes.

### 10.5 Use literal quotes from the prospect
Pull their own words from transcripts and drop them as italic quote callouts on at least three slides (the pain slide, the gap slide, and the close slide). Attribution: name + call date.

### 10.6 Currency matching
If the prospect is in a non-USD country, show USD as primary + local currency in brackets at the common exchange rate at build time. Example: `$68K (A$95K at 1.397)`. Check the rate the day of.

### 10.7 Spell tool and company names exactly
Auto-correct transcripts that mis-hear tool names. Verify spelling by searching the actual company site. A misspelled tool name on slide 10 kills trust faster than anything else on the deck.

## 11. FAQ construction — from their prior-call questions

The FAQ on the final slide should **not** be generic. It should answer the specific objections this prospect has raised on prior calls.

**How to build it:**

1. Open every prior call transcript.
2. Grep for: `what if`, `how do we know`, `I'm worried about`, `what happens when`, `is this realistic`, `what about`, question marks near concern-adjacent words.
3. Cluster the hits into 5-6 themes.
4. Write each FAQ summary to *lead with their own language*. Example: *"I'd certainly have a look at a leads-basis structure. What's our downside if it misses?"*
5. Answer in 3-5 sentences. Honest, specific, no fluff.
6. Each answer's last sentence should echo one element of the risk reversal architecture (at-cost / performance-only / cancel / recording).

**What not to do:**
- Don't invent objections that didn't come up. It makes the deck feel like a generic template.
- Don't answer more than 6 FAQs. More than 6 feels like you're protesting too much.
- Don't use the FAQ slide to make new claims you didn't earn earlier in the deck.

## 12. Iteration loop

A serious growth plan build takes 15-40 iterations over 4-10 hours with the prospect's data fresh in your head. Each round: small correction, apply, re-review.

### Correction categories (in order of frequency)

1. **Copy tightening.** Widow words, em dashes, too many words on a line. Apply the widow rule proactively — scan every block before claiming done.
2. **Number alignment.** When one number changes, many downstream references change. Always grep for the old number and fix every reference. E.g., changing a floor from $1,249 to $1,201 also changes `$14,988` (×12), `$43,788` (total paid/year), and the subtitle that says "~$44K."
3. **Positioning.** Prospect's brand vs yours; "genuine interest" vs "happy to chat"; "marketing lead" vs "account manager."
4. **Math clarity.** The prospect will push back on any number that doesn't feel defensible. Have the derivation ready. (e.g. *"150 sending accounts = 75 domains at 2 per domain. 100 active + 50 warming. Every 4 weeks we replace the oldest 25 domains."*)
5. **Spelling.** Tool names, people's names, company names. Triple-check.

### Anti-pattern
Don't apply fixes in isolation. When someone says "fix slide 17," scan slides 15 and 16 for the same class of issue. Pattern-match.

### Before declaring done at each iteration
- Grep for old numbers that should have been updated
- Spot-check 2-3 slides for widows
- Confirm any total row still sums correctly
- `grep -c "&mdash;" deck.html` must return 0

## 13. Pre-ship checklist

Before you send, share, or present the deck:

- [ ] Every number traces to a CLIENT / MARKET / TOOL / OFFER source (see §4)
- [ ] TAM screenshot embedded, captioned, dated
- [ ] Three-scenario P&L table with Conservative column at defensible cold-outbound close rate
- [ ] All currency bracketed for non-US prospects
- [ ] Total infrastructure sums correctly (if you have a line-item breakdown)
- [ ] No em dashes: `grep -c '&mdash;' deck.html` = 0
- [ ] No widow words (visual scan + `text-wrap: pretty` CSS applied)
- [ ] Tool names spelled correctly
- [ ] Prospect's literal quote on the close slide with attribution and date
- [ ] 5 qualification criteria consistent between where they're introduced and where they're restated
- [ ] At least one screenshot of an actual tool/dashboard per pillar
- [ ] FAQ section on the close slide answers objections from their actual prior calls
- [ ] Arrow-key navigation script works (test with arrow keys in a browser)
- [ ] Images load (every `<img src>` resolves)
- [ ] Mobile view is not broken (resize to 375px and scan)

## 14. Deployment

### Option A — GitHub Pages (free, public URL)
```bash
git init
git add .
git commit -m "Growth plan deck for [Prospect]"
gh repo create your-org/[prospect-slug]-growth-plan --public --source=. --push
gh api repos/your-org/[prospect-slug]-growth-plan/pages -X POST -f "source[branch]=main" -f "source[path]=/"
```
Live URL: `https://your-org.github.io/[prospect-slug]-growth-plan/` within ~2 minutes.

### Option B — Password protected (StatiCrypt)
```bash
# 1. Copy source to /tmp (StatiCrypt v3.5 overwrites input files)
cp index.html /tmp/to-encrypt.html

# 2. Encrypt
cd /tmp && npx staticrypt@3.5.4 to-encrypt.html \
  -p "YOUR_PASSWORD" \
  -d enc-out \
  --short --remember 30 \
  --template-color-primary "#7c3aed" \
  --template-color-secondary "#09090b"

# 3. Move encrypted output into your repo as index.html
cp /tmp/enc-out/to-encrypt.html ./index.html

# 4. Commit + push
git add index.html && git commit -m "Encrypt deck" && git push
```

**Password convention:** use the prospect's first name, lowercase. Memorable for them, not guessable for strangers.

**Never commit the unencrypted source to a public repo.** Either gitignore `.src.html` or keep the source in a separate private repo.

### Option C — Netlify / Vercel drop
Drag the folder into the Netlify or Vercel dashboard. Instant URL, their free tier is plenty.

### Option D — Local only
Open the HTML file directly in a browser. Works, but you're the only one who can see it.

## 15. A realistic build timeline

For a single prospect with 2-3 prior calls of transcripts on file:

| Phase | Time |
|---|---|
| Pull transcripts, emails, numbers into one doc | 30-45 min |
| TAM validation + screenshot | 15-30 min |
| First pass of all 21 slides | 2-3 hours |
| First review + corrections | 30-60 min |
| Second pass | 1-2 hours |
| Final polish, widow scan, pre-ship checklist | 30 min |
| Deploy | 10-15 min |
| **Total** | **5-8 hours** |

If you're spending more than 10 hours, you're probably over-polishing or missing data. If less than 4, you're probably shortcutting the research.

## 16. What to keep vs change per prospect

### Keep the same
- Design system (CSS, colors, typography, animations)
- Case studies slide (slide 12) — your proof is your proof
- Deploy workflow
- FAQ structure (but the content changes)
- Guarantee *structure* (the architecture), even if the specific numbers shift

### Change every time
- Every number on slides 1-4 (their situation)
- Slide 11 TAM math (their market)
- Slide 13 in-house cost breakdown (proportional to their scope)
- Slide 14 value stack (proportional to what you're delivering)
- Slide 17 ROI tiers (their close rate, their deal size)
- Slide 19 "if all this did was..." (their specific outcomes)
- Slide 21 FAQ content (their prior objections)
- Color/brand accent (optional, if you're co-branding)

## 17. Anti-patterns (don't do these)

1. **Showing a price before slide 16.** You lose the anchor effect.
2. **Hiding your infrastructure costs.** Modern prospects Google everything. If they find out you're marking up $X in tools by 400%, trust dies.
3. **Using the prospect's warm close rate in your Conservative column.** See §6.
4. **Making up objections in the FAQ.** Generic objections make the deck feel like a template. Use their real ones.
5. **Deploying without a password gate when the deck has numbers.** Your prospect's revenue, your case study clients' numbers — these shouldn't be indexed by Google.
6. **Em dashes.** See §10.1. No exceptions.
7. **Widow words.** See §10.2. No exceptions.
8. **"Partnership" or "trusted advisor" language on the pain slides.** Pain slides are for pain. Save the warm-and-fuzzy for the guarantee and close.
9. **Generic case studies.** One specific case study with real numbers beats five vague ones.
10. **Screenshots that show test data, placeholder names, or your own dashboard with no context.** Crop, annotate, caption.
